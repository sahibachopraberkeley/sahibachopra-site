PhD candidate in Management of Organizations. I study how new technologies — and right now, generative AI — reshape work, collaboration, and the organizations we build around them.

My research asks what happens when a technology stops being novel and becomes part of how work actually gets done. Adoption is never just a technical event; it is organizational and human. I study generative AI as one such moment, tracing its effects on productivity, evaluation, expertise, and the design of organizations themselves.

  • How does AI change collaboration and coordination at work?
  • What organizational infrastructure does AI at scale require?
  • When AI is involved in work, what does transparency demand?
  • Who captures the benefits — and how do we keep adoption equitable?

Job market paper

Effort Opacity: Generative AI and Reciprocal Effort in Collaborative Work

Sahiba Chopra

Generative AI can obscure how much human effort went into a piece of work. In collaboration, that makes AI-assisted contributions read as lower-effort and draw less reciprocal attention. I test this in open-source software with one observational study and two pre-registered experiments.

Which Ideas Attract Workers? Gender and Labor Supply in Startups

Solène Delecourt, Katelyn Cranney, Sahiba Chopra, Rembrand Koning

Revise & resubmit, Management Science

Across 52,000+ startups and a field experiment, we show how a startup's focus on women shifts male labor supply, implying a sizable compensation offset. Nominated for the Best Conference Paper and Research Methods Paper prizes, SMS 2023.

The Fabricated Front: Generative AI and the Opacity of Workplace Performance

Sahiba Chopra, Tom Van Nuenen, Pratik Sachdeva

Under review, The Paris Journal on AI & Digital Ethics

From 1,250 interview transcripts, we identify five mechanisms of opacity in workplace AI use and argue governance should manage involvement rather than mandate universal disclosure.

Whose Transparency Is It Anyway?

Sahiba Chopra, Genevieve Smith, Natalia Luka, Min Kyung Lee

Working paper

A mixed-methods study surveying 400 stakeholders with participatory design sessions, examining how different groups understand what AI transparency should mean.

The Technical Debt Tradeoff: Generative AI, Shipping Speed, and the Future Cost of Work

Sahiba Chopra, Dhruv Agarwal

Work in progress

Using de-identified workplace data, we ask whether generative AI changes how technical debt accumulates and what that means for long-run organizational capacity.
